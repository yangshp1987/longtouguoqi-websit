Architecture Solutions

Solution Overview

"Lacquer in the south, polychrome painting in the north": since the era of the Yingzao Fashi (Treatise on Architectural Methods), Chinese lacquer has been the first line of defence for timber architecture. Restoration follows the principle of "repair the old as the old" — the material must be natural raw lacquer, the technique must follow the traditional ground layer, and not one stage of ash or lacquer may be skipped. This concerns not only appearance, but the lifespan of the timber itself over a century.

We offer restoration units and timber contractors a three-in-one package of materials, technique and guidance: raw lacquer freshly tapped from mature trees in the current year and supplied by project batch; coarse, medium and fine ash matched to the ground layer procedure; and on-site guidance from provincial-level ICH artisans. Samples from every lacquer batch are retained and matched one-to-one with the restoration project files — able to stand up to inspection, and to time.

What is being restored is not a building, but a piece of history passed on to the next generation.

How We Solve

Our Approach

A complete path from requirement to delivery, with standards, records and traceability at every step.

SurveyCondition survey: assess timber decay and film damage, and determine the depth of intervention
Ground layerGround layer: coarse, medium and fine ash applied stage by stage in the traditional "one hemp, five ash" method
LacqueringLacquer restoration: multiple coats of natural raw lacquer, with colours restored to the original specification
InspectionQuality inspection: testing film thickness and adhesion, with acceptance documentation issued
MaintenanceOngoing maintenance: maintenance manual provided, with periodic review visits
